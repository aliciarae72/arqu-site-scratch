A scroll study

Three dots,
then a line
that doesn't stop.

The ellipsis is the request as it arrives: finite, punctuated, closed. The line is what we do with it. On this page it is also how you move — the dots on the left are the navigation, and the line draws itself as you scroll.

01 — It arrives

A submission lands as three things and a mess.

Email, attachments, a schedule of values someone built in a spreadsheet in 2019. Scroll and watch it come in — the pace is yours, which is the point. Nothing here plays on its own.

02 — The open market

Every carrier who could write it, at once.

Not a shortlist someone remembered. Scroll forward and the market fans out; scroll back and it collapses to the one you'd have called anyway.

03 — The program

Layers stack while you're reading this.

Primary, first excess, second. The tower builds in step with the scroll, so you can hold it half-built and look at it — which is the state it's actually in most of the time.

04 — Human in the loop

The part that used to get dropped.

A hand comes in over the machine's work and marks the one thing it got wrong. Drawn at the speed a person draws, with a fixed wobble — the same every time, so it never reads as a glitch.

06 — Open market

One risk, the whole market.

A single submission reaches out to every market that could write it. Scroll and it fans out; scroll back and it gathers again. Positions are a fixed hash, not random — the same page every time you load it.

07 — Programs

The whole market, one program.

The same drawing with time running backwards — that is the point, not a shortcut. Put the two side by side and the two halves of the business read without a word of copy.

08 — Living background

It reacts to you.

A field that breathes as you scroll and parts around your cursor. Move the pointer over it — the dots push away and light up. Sophisticated underneath, simple on the surface.

09 — Depth

Turned by the scroll, not a clock.

Three hundred and forty points on a sphere, projected by hand. Scrolling spins it; the pointer tilts it. No 3D library — the browser draws every point.

10 — Human in the loop

A person decides, then it binds.

Submissions run the lane, hold at the gate while a person looks at them, and leave bound. The hold is the product — the machine does not clear the gate on its own.
